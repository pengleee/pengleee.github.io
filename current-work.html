Scope ambiguity in LLMs

Ongoing work · Linguistic interpretation and evaluation

How do large language models resolve sentences with more than one possible scope interpretation? I am examining this question at the intersection of formal semantics, computational linguistics, and psycholinguistics.

The question

Every student read a book.

Did the students read one shared book, or could each have read a different one?

The broader question is how structure and context affect the reading a model selects. Cross-linguistic comparison is a further direction: corresponding sentences need not support the same readings or preferences.

Working approach

I am developing a framework for making interpretation choices explicit, separating a reading’s availability from a preference for that reading. The evaluation design needs to account for sentence form, supporting context, and the way a question is presented to the model.

  • Specify the readings and the contrasts each example is meant to test.
  • Distinguish contextual effects from effects of prompting and response format.
  • Relate model outputs to linguistic analyses and, where appropriate, human judgment tasks.

Current stage

This is ongoing work. The public material currently consists of a research sketch and questions guiding the design; no benchmark results or completed human study are presented here.
