Moving between languages

In a note from my time abroad, I described three stages: distancing myself from my first language, accepting Chinese again, and moving more freely between Chinese and English.

It is a very short list. What interests me now is the change in attitude it records. At one point, using another language seemed tied to putting distance between myself and Chinese. Later, that opposition became less useful.

排斥母语，完全去中国化
接受中文
自由切换中英文

Three stages recorded in my personal notebook

I would keep this as a description of my own experience, not a general account of what it means to live between languages. A brief note cannot tell us how common a pattern is, or why it happens.

It does leave me with a question worth returning to: when I change languages, what else changes—the audience I imagine, the things I find easy to say, or the relationship I have with what I am saying?

An edited reflection on an earlier notebook entry. This is personal writing, not an empirical study of bilingualism.
