Course project · Fall 2024

Linguistic markers of misinformation

Do lexical patterns, syntactic features, and sentiment help distinguish misinformation from credible political content?

Overview

Course
DSAN 5000, Georgetown University, Fall 2024
With
Jonah Lichtenthal
Data
LIAR: 10,240 short political statements fact-checked by PolitiFact (the dataset’s training file, split 70/30 for this project). News articles gathered through News API, Media Cloud and the All the News dataset were used for exploration only.
Materials

RepositoryProject report (PDF)Project website

Methods

  • Classification — a Random Forest on 1,000 TF-IDF word features, predicting LIAR’s six truthfulness labels.
  • Linguistic analysis — part-of-speech counts compared across labels, and the words the classifier relied on most.
  • Sentiment — VADER and a DistilBERT sentiment model, compared across truthfulness labels.
  • Exploratory structure — topic modeling and dimensionality reduction, including NMF and t-SNE.

Results and limitations

23%accuracy
0.22macro F1
22%majority-label baseline
3,072test statements

On 3,072 held-out statements the classifier reached 23% accuracy and a macro F1 of 0.22. Always predicting half-true, the most frequent label, scores 22% on the same split, so the word features add almost nothing. Part-of-speech counts showed no clear relation to the labels, and sentiment was compared across labels in count plots without a statistical test.

Patterns tied to dataset labels do not show whether an unfamiliar claim is true, and short statements are not news articles.

My contribution

In a team of two, I collected the data (News API, Media Cloud, All the News and LIAR) and wrote configurable news-gathering functions, built and evaluated the classifier, ran the part-of-speech and sentiment analyses and part of the exploratory analysis, wrote the report’s supervised learning, results and conclusion sections, and organized the project website. Jonah Lichtenthal did the data cleaning and topic modeling and wrote the remaining sections.

What it led to

The project sharpened a question I now pursue directly: what a measurable textual pattern shows about meaning, and what further evidence an interpretation needs.
