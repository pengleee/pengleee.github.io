Research

Meaning in context, and how to measure it in language models.

My interests are in formal semantics, pragmatics, computational linguistics, and psycholinguistics — in particular quantifier scope, discourse interpretation, the syntax–semantics interface, and the linguistic evaluation of language models.

Current project

ScopeShift

Independent research · July 2026 – present

Does a preceding question shift which construal of a scope-ambiguous sentence a language model expects? A pilot on 8 development items fixed the design; a frozen confirmation study on 120 new items and three models found a shift in the predicted direction in every model, together with clear limits on what the measure can show.

Questions behind the project

Availability versus preference. When a sentence has more than one reading, the grammar may make both available while context makes one easier to reach. Experiments need to keep these apart: a task that measures whether a sentence is judged true cannot by itself say which reading was taken.

Discourse and the question under discussion. Question-based theories of discourse predict that a hearer prefers the reading that answers the current question. Language models are a test case: does this sensitivity show up in a system trained on text alone, and what in its output would count as evidence that it does?

Measures as proxies. Continuation probabilities, truth judgments, and paraphrase choices each stand in for interpretation imperfectly. Much of my work so far has been about finding where a measure breaks, and reporting that alongside the effect.
