Research

Language structure, interpretation, and computational representation.

My interests connect questions about linguistic meaning with methods from Data Science and NLP. I am developing a research direction that brings formal accounts of language into conversation with human interpretation and the behavior of language models.

Meaning and structure

Formal semantics and the syntax–semantics interface provide a starting point for asking how sentence structure constrains interpretation. I am particularly interested in quantifier scope and ambiguity: when multiple readings are available, what makes one reading more accessible than another?

Interpretation and processing

Psycholinguistics offers a way to connect these questions with how people understand language. I am interested in the roles of context and language experience, and in how experimental tasks can distinguish possible interpretations from preferred ones.

Computational representations

In computational linguistics and NLP, my interests include linguistic representation in LLMs, distributional semantics, and information retrieval. A recurring question is what model behavior reveals about representation—and what remains unresolved when a model produces a plausible answer.

Current focus

Scope ambiguity in large language models brings these interests together. The work is ongoing; the current emphasis is on defining the question and evaluation design, rather than reporting established findings.
